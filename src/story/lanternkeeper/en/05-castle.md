Inside the gate, it was narrow.

Between high walls, the road turned and turned again. Every wall was pierced with arrow slits.

A citadel built to guard the Door.

The Door to the Far Side. The end of the Funeral Road. It stood at the very heart of this citadel.

Inside the lantern, two flames pressed against the glass. The flames of the Lantern Keepers gathered in the hall.

The Keeper walked the way those two pointed.

At the first corner, an arrow came.

A skeleton in full armor. Its armor was nothing like the soldiers' in the catacombs. Black and thick.

The garrison that had guarded the citadel.

Behind it, one with a shield stepped out. Dragging a sword as tall as a man.

The Keeper drew a breath.

There was not a whole part left on their body. Shoulder, side, back.

Still, the lantern was bright.

Flames gathered in the catacombs. Flames gathered in the root forest. Flames gathered in the hollow, in the hall. Hundreds of lights reached to the end of the passage.

*It's borrowed.*

I know, the Keeper answered inwardly.

Just a little longer, then.

---

Past the garrison, they came.

Snuffed Keepers.

At first they were gray. The same cloaks as in the hall.

After that, the color changed.

Purple.

The Keeper looked down at the hem of their own cloak.

The same color.

The cloaks of the ridge.

The ones who had come down this road over three hundred years. Who had handed their fire to an apprentice, come down to open the road, and never gone back.

The teacher's teacher. The teacher above that one.

They stood in the passage. Each holding a dead lantern.

The one in front drew a dagger.

The Keeper drew theirs too.

Their hands shook.

"……I'm sorry."

They cut it the way they had been taught.

With the very skill those ones had handed down, generation after generation.

The second one circled to the left.

It was the Keeper's habit. A habit they had been told countless times to fix. One handed down from these very people.

So they could read it.

The fourth one's cloak had rotted until only the color was left. The sixth one's still had sound threads. The closer the generation, the deeper down it had come.

On the hem of the seventh one's cloak, there was a place where the edge had been folded and sewn. The stitches were even and close.

The teacher's sewing.

The one who must have watched the teacher's lantern grow small from in front of the stone gate.

The Keeper caught their breath once before it. Then cut.

One by one, the purple cloaks settled to the floor. One by one, clear flames floated up. Once in the lantern, they all pressed against the same side of the glass.

Seven.

There was no eighth.

The Keeper looked to the end of the passage.

There should be one more.

---

A courtyard.

A wide place with no roof. Above, a dizzying dark.

On one wall were three soot lines.

The last one had never been finished. Halfway along, it slid downward.

Someone stood in front of that wall.

A purple cloak.

There was a mended patch on the left shoulder. The stitches were crooked.

The Keeper had sewn it.

The year they first held a needle.

The scythe almost slipped from their hand.

That person stood with their back turned. As if looking at the three lines on the wall.

A lantern hung from the right hand.

It was out.

"……"

A name rose to their throat.

They swallowed it.

That person turned around.

Inside the hood, there was no face.

Even so, the Keeper knew. From the way they stood. From the habit of tilting the head a little.

That person raised a scythe.

The hand that had taught the Keeper how to hold a scythe.

---

It was a fight they could only lose.

Everything the Keeper knew had come from that person.

When they raised the scythe, it was already blocked. When they tried to step in close, that person had already stepped back. When they circled left, a blade was already waiting on the left.

*Fix that habit of circling left. It can be read.*

They had never fixed it.

The back of a scythe struck their wrist. Their scythe went flying.

The blade swung back and passed in front of their throat.

If they hadn't thrown their head back, it would have been over. That person did not stop.

That was no longer the teacher.

It was the body the teacher had left. A body held by a soul that could not cross.

*If I don't, come down and send me off.*

That was what they had come for.

The Keeper stepped back and put their back to the wall.

The moves they knew could not win.

There had to be something the teacher had never taught.

There was.

Something learned on this road.

The Keeper set the lantern on the ground. At their feet.

A Lantern Keeper never lets go of the lantern. It was the first thing the teacher had taught.

That person's step stopped for half a beat.

The blade that had been aiming for the hand holding the lantern lost its target.

That half a beat.

With an empty hand, the Keeper caught that wrist. Drove in with their body. Their forehead touched the mended patch on the shoulder.

They drew the dagger across.

The cord parted.

---

The purple cloak grew light in the Keeper's arms.

They laid it down slowly.

The dead lantern slipped from the hand and rolled.

A flame floated up.

It was clear.

A flame that burned leaning a little to one side. Like the way that head tilted when smiling.

The Keeper looked at that flame a long time.

They said you could recognize a person by the shape of their flame alone. If you knew them well.

The Keeper recognized it.

"I came down."

No voice came out. They said it again.

"I came down. Late. It took me years."

The flame wavered.

They opened the lantern's little door.

The flame came in. Passed between the other flames and stood right beside the Keeper's fire.

The one who had given them that ember.

Then that flame, too, leaned toward the glass. The same way as the other Lantern Keepers.

Toward where the stolen pieces were.

The Keeper picked up the teacher's dead lantern. The glass was cracked. They tied it to their belt. Beside the Deputy's.

They stood in front of the three lines on the wall.

The third line, never finished.

*At the Door, put out the flame, whoever's it is.*

The Keeper put soot on their fingertip, and finished that line.

---

Beyond the courtyard, the way went down.

Wide stairs. Along them, three braziers.

They were out.

The Keeper shook out every drop of oil left in the lamp and lit them.

The first. The second.

When the third brazier caught, a voice came.

This time it did not come from a crack in the stone. It seemed to rise from below the stairs, from the whole of the dark.

A low, slow voice.

> I remember the day I took that child as my apprentice.
> The first thing I taught was this.
> At the Door, you put it out.

An Echo.

But it was not like the others. It was not words the dead had dropped along the way.

They were the words of someone still speaking.

> On the way down, lighting the braziers, that child died.
> A Lantern Keeper cannot cross alone.
> It was mine to send.

The Keeper looked down the stairs.

The amber light was there.

That was as much as the Keeper heard.

The voice remembered far more.

## Before the Door

I remember the day I took that child as my apprentice.

A small house on the edge of the kingdom.

Two vigil fires hung under the eaves. The fires of a couple taken by the plague.

Beneath them, a child sat alone.

Trimming the wicks with a knife the size of the child's palm.

"If the fire gets drowsy, trim the wick a little. Then it stands up again."

No one had asked, but the child said it.

I had come to take those two flames.

When I moved them into my lantern, the child asked,

"Where are you going?"

"To the Door."

"And then?"

"I put them out."

"And then?"

"They cross over."

The child thought for a long time.

"Then please put them out. Mom and Dad have waited a long time."

The child did not cry.

Until then, I had never had an apprentice.

The first thing I taught was this.

At the Door, you put it out.

The child understood at once. With the face of someone hearing again what they already knew.

---

The child learned well.

How to light a fire. How to see the cord. How to light the braziers.

The day a piece of the child's own soul was set into a lantern, the child cried a little. Not because it hurt, the child said. The child asked if that meant never going to Mom and Dad.

A Lantern Keeper cannot cross alone.

"I will send you off."

That is how I answered.

"And when you grow old and die, your own apprentice will send you. Don't worry."

I never thought the child might go before me.

The cloak was big. I meant to make a small one, but the child refused. "I'll grow into it soon."

So I gathered up the hem and tucked it into the belt. Every day.

There was one thing I never taught.

Don't step out of the light.

I thought there was no reason to teach it. The child was always inside my light.

---

The king died.

A great soul. A soul the whole land was holding on to.

I lit the fire before the tomb and told the soldiers,

We will return for him.

The child came back having given their knife to one of the soldiers. Said they had taught him how to trim the wick.

"What will you do without a knife?"

"We'll be right back, won't we?"

On the way down, lighting the braziers, that child died.

In the hall.

There were many wandering things. I fought up ahead. I pushed the light forward.

Behind me came the sound of iron rolling.

I looked back.

A small lantern sat on the brazier's ledge.

No one was beside it.

Three steps away, I could see the hem of a cloak.

The hem I had tucked into the belt had come loose.

---

A Lantern Keeper cannot cross alone.

It was mine to send.

I put that child's flame in the small lantern. Together with the lantern's fire.

I went down alone.

I stood before the Door. I held up that child's flame.

The flame leaned toward the Door.

It wanted to go. That was where Mom and Dad had gone. The ones I sent.

I drew a breath.

I had done it thousands of times.

Then a sound came out of my mouth.

At the Door, I called that child's name.

I don't know why. I didn't mean to. It was a taboo I had never once broken in my life.

The flame stopped.

The flame turned back to look at me.

The flame that had leaned toward the Door turned around, toward me.

I could not put it out.

---

I took the lantern and came back up.

A soul that has seen the Door can't stay in a fire again.

I taught that.

The fire grew smaller every day.

I took out a knife and trimmed the wick.

*If the fire gets drowsy, trim the wick a little. Then it stands up again.*

It did not stand up.

That child had never once said anything wrong.

The Deputy came. Asked me to put it out. Said he would do it.

He was right.

I took that fire into my hand.

I swallowed the dwindling fire. I thought it would hold if I burned it with my own soul.

It held for a few days.

Still it dwindled, so I fed it the fires of others.

Those who tried to stop me first. First the fire of the one who had walked beside me for twenty years.

I took everything that came down.

I pulled the fires from above too. As far as I could reach. Even the one I lit before the king's tomb.

The fire I promised to return for.

I know.

I know exactly what I am doing.

Putting it out is sending it off. Holding on is for my sake, not that child's.

Those are the words I said to others.

---

I stand with my back to the Door.

That fire inside me still leans toward the Door. Three hundred years now.

If I step aside, it will go.

If it goes, I will never see it again.

It seemed that if the fire grew big again, the child would open their eyes.

I know it cannot be. There is no way to bring back the dead. I know that better than anyone.

But as long as the fire is here, that child is here.

Three hundred years.

I still call that name.

Each time I call, the fire looks back.

Each time it looks back, it grows a little smaller.

Now it is smaller than a bean.

A light is coming down from above.

It lit all three braziers on the way. Like that child.

That lantern is bright.

It carries hundreds.

If I take it, I can hold on for a while.

---

## The One with His Back to the Door

Where the stairs ended was a wide, low room.

There were no walls. Only pillars.

In the middle of it, that man stood.

He was large.

The cloak that must once have been gray was nearly black. The hood was deep.

In his left hand he held a large lantern. It was out. The glass was whole. Inside, it was black, as if scorched.

And from his chest, light shone.

From inside the cloak, from inside the flesh.

It was amber.

There were three.

The two on either side were bright. Light made of dozens of fires gathered into one.

The one in the middle was small. Dim. Flickering as if about to go out.

*There are those who don't send what they carry, but keep it for themselves. They're called Devourers. As much as they swallow, their own soul is drawn into the lantern, until all that's left is a lantern that will never light.*

The teacher had told it like an old tale.

It was not an old tale.

Behind him was darkness.

Even when the lantern's light reached it, it lit nothing.

The Door.

Hundreds of flames inside the lantern pressed against the glass at once.

Toward it.

"I too once carried a lantern, as you do."

The voice from the stairs.

The Keeper gripped the lantern in both hands.

"Are you the Master?"

"I was."

The hood bowed a little. Looking at the Keeper's lantern.

"You've gathered so many."

Then he stopped.

"That flame."

The Master's voice dropped.

"The one that burns leaning to one side. I took that lantern. A few years ago."

The Keeper's hands froze.

"Your teacher, then."

The Keeper did not answer.

"You've brought them to the Door, so now you'll have to put them out."

The Master laughed. A sound like breath leaking.

"You won't be able to either."

---

The Master raised his right hand.

The fire in the lantern was torn loose.

The fire in the very middle. The Keeper's own fire. Stretching long toward the glass, thin as thread.

The Keeper's knees buckled.

Everything went dark before their eyes.

The Master did not come closer. He did not move a single step. He only stood and pulled. As he had for three hundred years.

The fire touched the glass.

A little further and it would be over.

*Never let the fire go out.*

They had no strength left to keep it.

But the fire stopped.

Through blurred eyes, the Keeper looked inside the glass.

The flames.

The hundreds of flames they had gathered had surrounded the Keeper's fire.

The king and the Queen on either side. The soldiers outside them. The people of the hollow, the bearers, the nameless ones from the cocoons.

One small flame clung at the very center. The one from the hollow, who had come up only to the Keeper's waist.

And right beside the Keeper's fire, a flame that burned leaning to one side.

They were holding on.

The ones before had come down with empty lanterns. There was no one to hold on to them. Not the teacher, either.

A heart that holds on adds no strength. It only refuses to let go.

Right now, that was enough.

"……Why won't it come?"

For the first time, the Master's voice shook.

The Keeper stood.

Held the lantern to their chest.

One step.

The fire was pulled. The flames held firm.

Another step.

---

The Keeper stood in front of the Master.

Up close, they could see the threads.

Hundreds of them.

Stretching out from the bright fires on either side, wrapping around the Master's body, scattering up through the whole citadel. Threads tying each swallowed lantern to its owner. The near end of the threads that could not be cut in the hall.

The flames of the Lantern Keepers inside the lantern trembled.

The Keeper raised the scythe.

The Master did not block. He had no arm to block with. His right hand was pulling, and his left held the blackened lantern.

The scythe passed.

From the left fire, one light broke away.

A small fire. A fire that had once burned in someone's lantern.

It flew to the Keeper's lantern and joined one of the flames. One gathered in the hall.

The two became one.

The Keeper cut. And cut again.

Light poured out. Dozens, hundreds. Some flew into the lantern seeking their owners, some flew up through the citadel, some crossed straight into the dark behind the Master.

The fires on either side grew smaller.

The Master's knees buckled.

Still he did not fall.

The middle fire remained.

With the light on either side cleared away, it showed how small that fire was.

Even as the Keeper watched, it shrank. The light on either side had been holding it up.

For the first time, the Master moved.

He let go of the blackened lantern. Cupped both hands over his own chest. Over that small fire.

"Not this child."

"……"

"If I send it, I'll never see it again."

The Keeper gripped the dagger.

Between that small fire and the Master there was a single thread. Thin. Worn and worn over three hundred years, until it was nearly invisible.

And yet tauter than any other.

The Keeper looked at that small fire.

Between the Master's fingers, it lay down.

Toward behind the Master.

Each time the Master's hands tightened, the fire tipped toward the hands for a moment. Then lay down again. And each time, it grew a little smaller.

"It wants to go."

The Keeper said.

The Master's hands shook.

"Look at it. It's toward the Door."

"……"

"There's no fire left to hold it up. Like this, it will fade away here. Without even crossing."

The Master did not answer. He knew it better than anyone.

"I was told it was the first thing you taught. At the Door, you put it out."

The Master looked down at his chest.

For a long time.

"……Yes. It was the first thing I taught."

His hands opened a little.

"That child understood at once."

The Keeper drew the dagger across.
