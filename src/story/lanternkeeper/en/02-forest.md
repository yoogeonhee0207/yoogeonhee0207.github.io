Where the stone stairs ended, the stone ended too.

There were no walls. No ceiling.

There were roots.

Roots as thick as an arm, as a body, as a house. Stretching down from above, from darkness into darkness, without end.

Nowhere was there a mark of human tools.

*No one made the road. It was there from the start.*

The teacher had said that once. That tombs and castles were only things people had set on top of it.

This was the Funeral Road as it truly was.

The Keeper stepped onto the roots.

About ten steps in, something wrapped across their face.

A web.

They raised the lantern.

As far as the light reached, it was all webs. Between every root and the next, white sheets hung layer upon layer.

And something hung in every one.

Cocoons.

From the size of a fist to the size of a person's head. Hundreds. Maybe more.

They held the light to the nearest one.

Inside the white threads, something flickered faintly.

Their breath stopped.

It was a flame.

---

They split the cocoon with the scythe.

As the threads parted, a small, dim flame crawled out.

It trembled a moment, then came toward the lantern. Once inside the glass, it grew still.

They split another. And another.

All of them were souls.

A soul that has lost its vigil fire is pulled down toward the Door. It goes down even though it cannot cross alone.

But the road was blocked.

Three hundred years of souls coming down had caught here in the roots and piled up. And something had wrapped them up, one by one.

To eat.

Overhead came a dry sound.

They looked up.

A spider. One the size of a dog was climbing down the roots. Another behind it. And more behind that.

There was no cord to cut. It was a beast grown on souls.

The scythe went first.

Two legs fell away. The spider spat thread. Their left arm stuck fast to a root.

They cut the thread with the dagger and rolled. They did not let go of the lantern.

After cutting down three, they were gasping for breath.

They raised their head.

Hundreds of cocoons were still left.

The Keeper shifted their grip on the scythe.

They could not leave even one behind.

There was no fire here to leave them with.

*Take every soul you carry all the way to the Door.*

Split, gather, cut. Split, gather.

They could not tell how long it went on.

The lantern grew brighter and brighter. It reached three times as far as it had in the catacombs.

The lantern weighed the same.

But their arms trembled.

The glass was full of flames. People whose names and faces they did not know. People who had died when the kingdom fell, with no fire to be lit for them.

People no one had come for in three hundred years.

---

An arrow drove into a root.

Right beside their ear.

The Keeper twisted and slipped behind the root. A second arrow grazed the lantern's glass.

At the edge of the light, they stood.

Skeletons. Like the soldiers of the catacombs, but different.

The armor was thinner. The crest on the chest was different. Bows and short swords instead of spears.

The way they stood was different too.

The stance of those who walk surrounding something. With the middle left empty.

Five came at once.

Fast. Much faster than the soldiers.

The Keeper fought with their back to a root. Caught a sword on the scythe, stepped in close, and cut the cord. One. Two.

The third one's sword raked across their shoulder.

It burned.

Gritting their teeth, they cut the fourth one's cord. The fifth was backing away with its bow raised, but when the Keeper drew near, it lowered the bow.

Just before the cut, a voice came.

From a crack in the roots. A low woman's voice.

> The Queen waited for word.
> Just one word: that His Majesty had crossed.
> No word came, and the people began to leave the kingdom.

An Echo.

The Queen.

The Keeper looked down at the lantern. In the middle of the glass, beside their own fire, was a large, dim flame. The king's flame, gathered in the catacombs.

That flame was still leaning downward.

The fifth one was still standing there with its bow lowered.

It did not charge. It was looking at the middle of the lantern.

The Keeper cut the cord. The flame went straight to the large, dim one.

A little further down, they heard once more.

> The Queen breathed her last, and even her vigil fire went out.
> She rose on her own and went down the road.
> We followed her, dead.

The Keeper stopped walking.

She went down, it said. Down this road.

Then where was she now?

That was as much as the Keeper heard.

The voice remembered far more.

## The One Who Went Down Searching

We were the Queen's guard.

After His Majesty closed his eyes, the Queen climbed the walls every day.

She looked toward the tomb beneath the palace. Where the Funeral Road begins.

The Queen waited for word.

Just one word: that His Majesty had crossed.

Only when that word came could His Majesty's name be spoken again. Only then could a new king be crowned.

"They say he promised to return for him."

The Queen asked that once.

"Yes. The Master of the Lantern Keepers is said to have told us so."

"Then he will come. A man of few words keeps the words he speaks."

A year passed.

Five years passed.

No word came, and the people began to leave the kingdom.

It was a land with no one to send off the dead.

Nearly all the Lantern Keepers had gone down with that procession. They said one was left, on the ridge. One person could not tend a whole land.

All that grew were the fires under every eave.

The Queen did not leave.

"His Majesty is still down there."

Nor did we.

---

The Queen grew old.

She climbed the walls until her hair was white. In the end we helped her up.

Around then, word came up that the fire at the tomb beneath the palace had gone out.

From that night, the buried came out of their graves.

When we went out with torches, there was nothing. But in the morning, people were dead.

Taken by something unseen.

We lit fires from our neighbors' flames for the dead. It did no good.

Overnight the fires stretched long and went out. Starting from the lower town, nearest the tomb. Leaning toward the tomb beneath the palace.

The dead of a house whose fire went out rose the next night.

Carts lined up to leave through the city gate. This time almost no one stayed.

Still, the Queen climbed the walls.

On her last day, too, she was looking toward the tomb.

The Queen breathed her last, and even her vigil fire went out.

We lit that fire. From an old fire still left in the palace. We filled the oil with our own hands.

One night that fire stretched long. Toward the tomb beneath the palace. And went out.

By then we were already old. We ran to light it again, but though the fire caught, she did not come back.

That night, the Queen rose.

She rose on her own and went down the road.

She did not come to us. Her footsteps went straight down under the palace, to the stairs.

She was going to find him.

We could not stop her. We had no eyes to stop her with. We could not see her. We only heard the door open and her footsteps going down.

We took torches and followed her down.

Beneath the stairs, we fell one by one. Without knowing what took us.

We followed her, dead.

Only after death could we see her.

---

The Queen passed by His Majesty's tomb.

She did not stop.

His Majesty was in that chamber. We knew. We saw the soldiers, nothing but bones, bowing there.

But the Queen did not know. She believed the one she sought was further down. That since he had not crossed, he must be somewhere near the Door.

We could not tell her.

After death our words grew faint. Only one thing was left. We must follow.

And we came here.

A flame hangs from every root.

The road was blocked, and no one could cross.

The spiders are wrapping them, one by one.

For the first time, the Queen stopped.

She looked up at the hanging flames. One by one. As if searching among them for His Majesty.

Then it came down from above.

We drew our swords.

They could not reach.

A spider swallowed the Queen.

Now it wears her face.

We could not protect her.

---

Since then, we circle between these roots.

Leaving the middle empty. The place where the one we served once stood.

It happened once, long ago.

A Lantern Keeper came up from below. His lantern was out.

His cloak was not gray. It was a color we had never seen.

Someone who had come down from above, lost his fire, and was going back.

He groped his way up the roots. He could not see us in front of him. Without a fire, you cannot see.

He said,

"The Master stands blocking the Door."

We did not know who he was saying it to. He must have been on his way to tell those above.

He never reached them.

And very long after, a light came down from above.

The lantern was lit.

We raised our bows. All that was left in us was that we must, when we saw a light.

The last of us lowered the bow.

It had seen who was inside that light.

The large, dim flame in the middle of the glass.

The one she had been searching for was coming down from above.

## The Queen's Face

Where the roots spread wide, there stood a stone brazier.

Up to here, human hands had reached. It was out.

The Keeper lit it from the lamp. Red light spread between the roots.

On the bark of a root beside the brazier were three soot lines.

The Keeper touched them.

The teacher had come this way too.

The teacher must have seen these cocoons.

There was not a single mark of one being split.

The teacher had not gathered them.

The teacher must have meant to open the road first, and gather them on the way back. If the teacher didn't come back, the next one could gather them.

The next one was the Keeper.

And after the Keeper, there was no one.

Beyond the brazier's glow, where the roots were tangled thickest, something moved.

Something very large.

---

It came down over the roots.

Eight legs braced on eight roots. A body the size of a cart.

And where its head should have been, there was a face.

A human face.

An old woman's face. Hair all white. Eyes closed.

The face said nothing.

The Keeper took a step back.

Inside the lantern, something struck the glass.

They looked down.

The king's flame.

The large, dim flame was pressed flat against one side of the glass. The side it had been leaning toward ever since the catacombs.

It was not downward.

It was toward that face.

Only then did the Keeper understand.

For three hundred years, the king had waited above. The Queen had searched below.

The two had been on the same road.

The spider leapt.

The Keeper rolled aside. A root snapped off.

Thread poured down. Their ankle was bound. They cut it with the scythe and stood, and this time cocoons fell from above.

From every burst cocoon a spiderling crawled out.

They raised the lantern high.

It was bright. The light of hundreds of gathered flames.

*That light isn't yours. It's borrowed.*

Still, right now, they had to borrow it.

The little ones shrank from the light. The Keeper ran straight through them.

Under the big one's legs.

From the underside of its belly, a light shone. From inside the shell. A single thin, white flame, wrapped in thread, swaying.

Swallowed, but never fully digested.

For three hundred years.

A leg came down on the Keeper. It felt as if their side had burst.

No breath would come.

Still, their hand rose.

The dagger slid into a gap in the shell. It found the thread wound around the flame.

Cut it in one stroke.

The spider flipped over without a sound.

Its legs curled in. The face crumbled away, as if melting. What was left in its place was the head of a beast with eight eyes.

The thin, white flame floated up.

It stopped in front of the lantern.

As if peering through the glass.

The Keeper opened the lantern's little door.

"He's here."

Their throat was tight.

"He was waiting for you up above."

The flame came in.

Past hundreds of flames, to the middle. Beside the large, dim flame.

The two flames stood side by side.

For the first time, the king's flame stood straight.

It no longer leaned in any direction.

---

Overhead came the sound of thread unraveling.

The cocoons.

The webs were rotting away. The cocoons that remained split open one by one.

Flames floated up.

Ten. Fifty. A hundred.

The whole root forest filled with small flames. As if stars had come out in the dark.

The Keeper stood with the lantern's door open.

The flames came down. One by one, in a line.

They did not close the door until the last one was in.

Blood seeped from their side. They tore their cloak and bound it.

They raised the lantern.

Their arms trembled. Still they held it up.

The root road went on downward.

But beside it there was a hole.

Not like the holes the roots had bored. It was square. Wooden posts held it up.

There were pickaxe marks on the walls.

A tunnel dug by people.

On the post at its mouth, three soot lines had been drawn.

And from the darkness inside came a very small sound.

The sound of someone holding their breath.
