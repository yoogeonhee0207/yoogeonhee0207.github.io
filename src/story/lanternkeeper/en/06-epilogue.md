title: "The Black Lantern"

---
The thread parted.

There was no sound.

The Master's hands opened.

A small fire slipped out of his chest.

Smaller than a bean. It looked as if a single breath would put it out.

That fire stayed on the Master's palm.

The Master's lips moved.

He made no sound.

This time, he did not call.

The Master turned around.

For the first time in three hundred years, toward the Door.

He held out the fire on his palm before the dark.

And breathed out.

As he had taught.

The fire went out.

It had not gone out. It had crossed.

The Master fell to his knees.

The blackened cloak settled to the floor. There was nothing inside it. No bones, no earth.

A man who had stood for three hundred years on what he had swallowed. When all he had swallowed was gone, nothing was left.

A lantern rolled across the floor.

The one the Master had held in his left hand.

The large lantern, black inside.

Then a voice came.

From beside that lantern. A low, slow voice.

> Three hundred years.
> I still call.
> Saebyeol.

An Echo.

It was the first time the Keeper had heard that name.

After the crossing.

Now it was a name that could be spoken.

---

Above, it grew bright.

The Keeper looked up.

Fires.

The fires freed from the Master were still scattering.

Some went straight into the dark. The flames of the dead that had been swallowed. Souls taken, lantern and all, while being carried.

When they touched the dark, they went out on their own.

They said a soul held in a fire tries to slip free on its own at the Door. That putting it out is only letting it go quietly.

The flames held for three hundred years did not wait.

Some rose upward.

Up through the citadel, toward the hall. To the Snuffed Keepers the Keeper had passed without being able to cut free.

Stolen pieces going home to their owners.

Even with their pieces returned, they cannot cross alone.

A Lantern Keeper must be brought by another Lantern Keeper.

The Keeper would have to come down again. To bring them one by one.

The Keeper watched the rising fires to the end. Fixed in their eyes where each one went.

Then looked down at their own lantern.

The glass was full.

These came first.

---

The Keeper stood before the dark.

The Door.

There was nothing to see. Neither cold nor warm. No wind.

Only, every flame in the lantern leaned that way.

They opened the lantern's little door.

One flame came out first.

A nameless flame from a cocoon.

The Keeper took it on their palm.

They did as the teacher had taught. Something no one on the ridge had been able to do in three hundred years.

Drew a breath,

and blew.

The flame went out.

Their palm was warm for a moment. Then it grew light.

Next.

They sent off the flames of the root forest. People who had died when the kingdom fell.

They sent off the flames of the hollow.

The one who had gripped a pickaxe. The one who had carried a bundle.

The flame that had worn the apron and the small flame came out together. They would not part.

The Keeper blew on both together.

The Hollow Warden's dim flame came out last. As if waiting until it saw that everyone else had gone.

"Thank you for all you bore."

They blew.

The four bearers came out in step.

Those who had guarded the citadel.

The guards.

The soldiers.

Among them was one flame in a particular hurry. Trying to come out a little ahead of the rest.

The Keeper remembered that flame. The skeleton that threw down its bow and ran at them bare-handed.

"Go on ahead. Someone will be waiting for you."

They did not know who would be waiting. They just wanted to say it.

They blew.

---

The large, dim flame and the thin, white flame were left.

The two came out together.

The Keeper cupped both hands to take them.

For three hundred years, one had waited above, and one had searched below.

"We're late."

They said again what they had said in the catacombs.

"You can go now."

They blew on both together.

The two flames went out at once.

Now someone could call those two by name. If anyone who remembered them was left.

Clear flames remained in the lantern.

The Lantern Keepers.

The one gathered in the hall. The Deputy. The seven who came before, in purple cloaks.

Their freed pieces had come back, and all of them were whole.

The Keeper untied the broken lantern from their belt and set it before the Deputy's flame.

"I know you were holding the way."

They blew.

They sent off those who came before, one at a time.

As flames, there was no telling who was how many generations back. The Keeper did not ask. They only bowed their head to each one.

And one was left.

---

A flame that burned leaning to one side.

It was right beside the Keeper's fire.

It did not come out.

Because the Keeper had not held out their hand.

*You won't be able to either.*

Their hand would not move.

Put it out, and it crosses.

Once it crosses, you can never see it again.

They had waited years. Come all the way down this road. And only just found it.

By the shape of the flame alone, it was that person. Even the habit of tilting the head was the same.

They could carry it back up just like this.

Set it on the shelf in the house on the ridge, fill the oil, see it every day.

No.

They could not. This flame had seen the Door. A soul that has seen the Door can't stay in a fire again. Carry it up, and it would grow smaller every day.

Until it was the size of a bean.

The Keeper looked back at the Master's black cloak.

He had stood here too.

In this very place.

Holding the same thing.

*The third is the hardest.*

The teacher had said that and smiled.

Now the Keeper understood.

Why it was hard.

And why the teacher had smiled.

Because it is something you do even though it's hard. Because the teacher knew that someday an apprentice would do it for them.

*If I don't, come down and send me off.*

The Keeper held out their hand.

The flame came out and settled on their palm.

It was warm.

"I'll do it the way you taught me."

Their throat closed, and the words broke off.

"Because you taught me well."

They could say no more.

They drew a breath.

And blew.

The flame went out.

Their palm went cold.

The Keeper looked down at their empty hand a long time.

Then raised their head, and called that name into the dark.

For the first time in years.

Once.

There was no answer.

No one looked back.

That was enough.

---

Only one fire was left in the lantern.

Their own.

It was light.

Like the dawn they had left the house on the ridge.

Then, it had been so light their hand felt empty.

Now it was not.

They picked up the large lantern from the floor.

The Master's lantern.

Black inside. There was a wick. Some hardened oil was still left.

They took out the lamp. The oil had run out, but the fire could still be passed from its wick.

They touched the fire to the black wick.

It did not catch.

They touched it again.

It did not catch.

The fire touched it and nothing happened. It did not even scorch.

The Keeper carried the lantern to the dark.

Held it out.

Nothing leaned. Nothing pulled.

The Door did not take it.

Just as in the teacher's old tale. While he swallowed, that man's soul had gone into this lantern without remainder. And become a thing that would never light.

The black lantern.

This was him.

The Keeper could not leave it behind.

A Lantern Keeper must be sent off by another Lantern Keeper.

Even without knowing how.

They tied the black lantern to their belt. Beside the teacher's cracked lantern.

And turned around.

---

The way up was bright.

The braziers were lit.

Three on the stairs. Thirty-some in the hall.

The fires they had lit coming down were still burning.

With nothing pulling at them now, the fires did not go out.

In the middle of the hall, a small lamp sat on a brazier's ledge.

The Keeper slowed in front of it.

They did not take it. This was where that lamp belonged.

There were no webs in the root forest. Every root was clear.

On the heap of stones in the catacombs, they stopped.

The small knife lay there still.

A blade two finger joints long. Too small to be a soldier's knife.

This time they picked it up.

They looked over the blade. Short, thin, rounded at the tip.

A knife for trimming wicks.

A Lantern Keeper's knife. Made to fit a child's hand.

The same size of hand as the small lamp in the hall.

They put it inside their coat.

---

When they came out through the stone gate, it was dawn.

There was no telling how many dawns it had been.

The village was awake.

Someone stood in the doorway of every house. Like the day they left.

One thing was different.

The fires hung under the eaves.

Not held in hands. Not clutched to chests.

Hung in their places, burning straight.

There were fires under empty eaves too. Wherever a fire had gone out, a neighbor's fire had been passed on to it.

They were not the fires of souls that had come back.

At the end of the village, the barn door had been nailed shut. Boards on boards on boards.

The ones that had risen in the meantime. When the sun comes up, they fall. The people had dragged the fallen ones inside.

Cutting the cords was a Lantern Keeper's work. Work for tonight.

Even so, the people had lit fires under the empty eaves.

To light the place for coming home.

No one spoke.

As the Keeper passed, the people bowed their heads.

The same as the day they left.

A little different. They did not look away.

Beyond the wall, in the doorway of that house, stood the child.

The child pointed at the bowl under the eaves.

"I didn't let it go out."

This time, out loud.

The Keeper crouched down in front of the child.

Like last spring.

"The road is open."

The child's eyes went wide.

"So Grandma's going?"

"She's going. I'll take her. After a little rest."

The child looked up at the eaves. At Grandma's fire. At the fires beside it.

"All of them?"

"All of them."

Three hundred years' worth.

There was no knowing how many times they would have to go down and come back.

"After she goes,"

the child asked,

"can I call Grandma's name?"

For a long while, the Keeper could not answer.

"Yes."

Their voice cracked.

"Then you can call it as much as you like."

---

The house on the ridge was just as they had left it.

The fires on the shelves were burning. The souls of those who had risen this autumn.

Not one had gone out.

Someone had filled the oil.

There were muddy footprints on the threshold.

A threshold no one in the village had crossed in three hundred years.

The Keeper looked at those footprints a long time.

They untied the lanterns from their belt.

The teacher's cracked lantern they hung beside the doorpost. Under the three soot lines.

The black lantern they set at the end of the shelf.

Among the burning fires, only it was dark.

They took the small knife from their coat.

They opened the black lantern's little door and trimmed the very tip of the hardened wick.

That is how you raise a dwindling fire. Every Lantern Keeper knows it.

They touched the lamp's fire to it.

It did not catch.

The Keeper set the knife down beside it.

Whether it could ever be lit again, there was no knowing.

The teacher had never taught it. Perhaps no one had ever tried.

Still, they did not throw it away.

A Lantern Keeper must be sent off by another Lantern Keeper.

Someday.

Outside the window, it was growing light.

On the graves on the hill, fires were being lit one by one. The villagers had gone up to light them.

The Keeper set their own lantern down in front of the shelf.

A single fire burned straight.

They had not let it go out.

They had taken them all the way.

They had put them out.

And still, there were people left to take.
