The roots ended, and stone came again.

This time it was wide.

Even with the lantern raised, the walls on either side could not be seen. Nor the ceiling. Only the smooth stone floor stretched ahead underfoot.

There was a smell of ash.

Old ash. The smell of ash that had been wet and dried countless times.

Ten steps on, something caught at the edge of the light.

A stone brazier.

Chest high. Much bigger than the ones in the catacombs. It was out.

Beyond it, another. Beyond that, another.

The braziers stood in two rows. Down both sides of the road, evenly spaced, into the dark without end.

*To carry a great soul, light the braziers along the way first.*

This was that way.

The Keeper stood in front of the first brazier.

Moved the fire to the lamp, touched it to the bottom of the brazier.

The hardened oil melted and the fire rose. A low, red fire. In its light, the next brazier appeared.

They went to the next brazier.

Lit it.

And the next.

Each time a brazier was lit, the hall grew one stretch longer.

Three hundred years ago, someone had done this too. Holding one lamp, one stretch at a time.

---

In front of the seventh brazier, something lay across the road.

Two long poles. A wide board laid between them. Rotten cloth hanging off one side.

A bier.

It was empty.

The empty bier of a procession that had left the one it would carry above, and come down to light the road first.

Beside it, something stood up.

It carried a load on its back. A chest bigger than its own body. The straps dug into its shoulder blades.

A bearer.

There were four. All four still carried their loads. Not once in three hundred years had they set them down.

The one in front twisted round.

The dagger stuck in the chest.

It would not come out.

The bearer was guarding with its back. Just as it had in life. To protect the load.

An elbow flew. The Keeper's jaw snapped sideways.

The Keeper let go of the dagger and stepped back. Switched to the scythe.

From the front, it could not reach.

They circled behind the brazier. Before the bearer could turn, they slipped in at its side and cleared away the cord.

The load fell first. Then the bones crumbled.

The chest broke open and what was inside spilled out.

Oil flasks. Dozens. All dried up.

Oil for the braziers.

When all four were down, the Keeper pulled out the dagger.

Four flames came into the lantern. Even inside, they stayed close together. Like people who had walked in step.

Then the lantern dimmed.

Something was clinging to the glass.

Moths.

Dozens, each the size of a palm. Covering the glass with ash-gray wings.

The Keeper brushed them off. More came. At every lit brazier, moths gathered in clouds. Drawn by the fire.

The Keeper covered one side of the glass with the hem of their cloak.

The light was cut in half.

On the covered side, there was only the light of the braziers.

---

It was after the twentieth brazier.

Someone was standing in the dark ahead.

A person.

Wearing a hood. Wrapped in a cloak. A lantern in one hand.

The Keeper stopped.

Their breath stopped.

"……Teacher?"

No answer.

They took a step closer. The brazier's light touched the cloak.

It was gray.

The teacher's cloak was purple.

And the lantern in that hand was out.

The glass was broken. The wick had hardened black.

Inside the hood, there was no face.

A Snuffed Keeper.

It moved.

Fast.

A dagger came. A dagger just like the Keeper's. At just the same angle. To the very place where the cord that ties body to soul is cut.

The Keeper blocked. Their wrist went numb.

They swung the scythe. It had already stepped back. As if it had known the Keeper would swing.

Someone who had learned the same things.

Someone who had sworn the same vows.

They clashed three times, and on the fourth, the Keeper cut the cord.

The gray cloak settled to the floor.

The dead lantern rolled across the stone.

Above the gray cloak, a flame floated up.

It was clear. Not dim like the other flames. They said a Lantern Keeper's soul keeps its shape even in death.

Once it came into the lantern, the flame pressed straight against the glass.

The downward side.

Toward the end of the hall.

It was not like the way the king's flame had leaned. It was being pulled so hard the glass hummed.

The Keeper picked up the dead lantern from the floor.

*A Lantern Keeper whose lantern goes out isn't dead. They've lost their way. They can be lit again from another lantern.*

That was true, they said, for one who lost their way while still alive. The moment you touched the fire to it, they came back.

This one was not alive.

The Keeper knew it, and still took out the lamp. Their hands hurried.

They moved the fire from their own lantern and touched it to that black wick.

The fire caught.

A small, yellow fire.

They waited.

Nothing happened.

It was only fire. A fire that burns oil and wick. It did not swell as if drawing breath, the way a fire did when lit in a bowl under the eaves.

The dead cannot be lit again. The Keeper knew that.

But even the piece was gone.

The piece of that person that should have been held in this lantern.

Someone had taken it.

The Keeper looked again at the direction the flame inside was being pulled.

Its own piece was down there.

---

At the thirty-somethingth brazier, the Keeper stopped walking.

Something lay on the floor in front of it.

A lamp.

Lying on its side. About three steps from the brazier.

It was small.

Much smaller than the Keeper's lamp. The handle carved to fit a child's hand.

In front of the other braziers there was nothing. Only in front of this one.

A voice came.

From a crack in the brazier's stone. A tired voice.

> The Master was the sternest of us all.
> That child followed behind the Master, lighting the braziers.

The Master. That child.

The ones the soldier of the catacombs had spoken of.

The same voice went on.

> It was in this hall. The Master was fighting up ahead.
> That child took one step out of the light.

The Keeper looked down at the lamp.

Three steps.

From the brazier to the lamp, three steps.

*Don't chase them out of the light.*

That was as much as the Keeper heard.

The voice remembered far more.

---

## Those Who Tried to Stop Him

We were Lantern Keepers.

We wore gray cloaks and took the kingdom's dead to the Door.

The Master was the sternest of us all.

He did not smile. He spoke little. He kept the three vows more heavily than anyone.

We saw many who hesitated at the Door. One holding a mother's flame, one holding a friend's. One standing with shaking hands, unable to put it out.

At such times, the Master would go to their side and say,

"Putting it out is sending it off. Holding on is for your sake, not theirs."

When they heard that, everyone put it out.

The Master himself never once hesitated. He put out thousands of flames. He put out his own teacher's flame.

That was the kind of man he was.

That child followed behind the Master, lighting the braziers.

Twelve, maybe thirteen. Even we did not know where the Master had found the child. One day the child was simply there beside him.

The cloak was too big, and the hem dragged. The Master always gathered it up and tucked it into the belt.

Every time we saw that hand, we were startled.

We wondered if that man had such hands.

---

It was in this hall.

It was the king's procession. A road for carrying a great soul, so not a single brazier could be missed.

That child carried the lamp and lit the braziers. That was the apprentice's work.

We widened the light on either side.

There were always wandering things below on the road. There are always people who die without a fire. That day there were especially many.

The Master was fighting up ahead.

The Master's light went forward. Our light followed it.

That child was behind, lighting a brazier.

The child's lantern was small. Its light reached two steps. Our light had gone forward, following the Master.

Some of us heard the sound. Iron rolling across stone.

No one looked back. What lay ahead was urgent.

That child took one step out of the light.

We did not see it. We worked it out afterward. The lamp lay three steps away from the brazier. Without the lamp, the next brazier couldn't be lit.

The child's own lantern was still set on the brazier's ledge.

There was no scream.

When we looked back, there was only a small lantern sitting beside the brazier.

The Master came running.

It was the first time we had seen that man run.

---

A Lantern Keeper cannot cross alone.

Because a piece of their soul is in the lantern. Another Lantern Keeper must take that soul and the lantern's fire together to the Door and put them out.

No one asked who would do it.

The teacher does it. That was the law.

The Master carried that child's flame down to the Door.

Holding that small lantern, alone.

We waited in this hall. No one follows a teacher on the road of sending off an apprentice.

By the law, it should not have taken long.

Half a day passed.

And came back.

Without putting it out.

The flame was still inside the small lantern.

We could not say a word.

We could not believe the Master had failed to put it out. So we thought there must be some other reason.

There was no reason.

The Master set the lantern down in front of his knees and sat. He sat like that for days.

The fire grew smaller every day.

Every Lantern Keeper knew it. A soul that has seen the Door can't stay in a fire again. It never quite holds to any fire, and even if you pour in oil, it dwindles. In the end it does not even cross; it fades away.

The Master had taught us that.

The Master took out a knife and trimmed the wick. A little. Then a little more.

The fire did not stand up.

---

We stood before the Master. The Deputy at the front.

The Deputy was the Master's right hand. The one who had walked beside him for twenty years.

"Please, put it out."

The Master did not answer.

"If we go to the Door now, it's not too late. I'll go with you."

The Master only watched the fire.

"If you can't, I'll put it out."

We tried to stop him. The Deputy reached out to put it out.

The Master grabbed it first.

And turned his back.

We did not see what he did.

When he turned around, the small lantern was empty.

From deep in the Master's chest, a small light shone. It was amber.

The Master swallowed that fire.

The Deputy fell to his knees.

"……How could you?"

The Master raised his head.

We will never forget those eyes.

They were a person's eyes. Eyes that knew exactly what they had done.

That was more frightening.

---

That light did not last long either.

After a few days it grew faint again.

The Master looked at us.

At our lanterns.

The Deputy stood in his way.

The Master raised his hand.

The fire in the Deputy's lantern stretched long. Toward the Master. As if someone were drawing in a breath.

And went out.

After that, he took our lanterns, one by one.

When the lantern goes out, you can't see. Your strength can't reach anything.

We called to each other in the dark of the hall. One by one, the voices answering grew fewer.

While we were alive, we could still walk. A few went up to warn those above. None made it.

There was no one to send us off, either.

So we died and remained here.

Having lost our lanterns, we cannot leave the Master's side.

The pieces taken from us are in there. They pull at us. Two pieces of one soul seek each other harder than anything.

The Deputy still stands barring the hall.

So that no one becomes like the Master.

The Deputy came to believe it. If even the sternest of us broke at the Door, then anyone who carries a lantern will.

So no one must ever be allowed to stand before the Door.

---

Since then, light has come down a few times.

Once a generation. One lantern at a time. Cloaks of a different color from ours.

The Deputy blocked the way. Told them to turn back. That below, the Master stands blocking the Door.

One of them lost his lantern there and groped his way back up.

A few slipped past the Deputy. The Deputy cannot chase them. Just as we cannot leave the Master's side, he cannot leave his place at the end of the hall.

Those who slipped past did not come back either.

The light that came a few years ago stood in front of this brazier for a long time.

It looked at the lamp on the floor. Did not pick it up.

It lit the brazier. Drew something on the pillar.

That fire did not last. Pulled from below, it went out a while after that light passed.

And now another light has come down.

Lighting every brazier, skipping not one.

Like that child.

Now it is this brazier's turn.

---

## The End of the Hall

The Keeper picked up the small lamp.

It was light.

The oil had all dried up. Half the wick was left.

They stood it upright on the brazier's ledge.

Then lit the brazier with their own lamp.

The fire rose.

There was a thin layer of soot around the rim. Not old.

Someone had lit this brazier before. A few years ago.

They went around the brazier and looked at the pillar.

Three soot lines.

The Keeper stood before them a long time.

What had the teacher thought here?

Seeing this small lamp.

Seeing those three steps.

*Don't chase them out of the light.*

The teacher had never once smiled when teaching that. Other things the teacher taught with a smile, but not that.

The Keeper went on to the next brazier.

---

At the last brazier, they heard that voice once more.

> Having lost our lanterns, we cannot leave the Master's side.
> The Deputy still stands barring the hall.
> So that no one becomes like the Master.

The Deputy.

The Keeper looked ahead, lamp in hand.

When they lit the last brazier, the end of the hall came into view.

A gate.

A tall door plated with iron. A citadel gate. Half open.

In front of it, it stood.

A gray cloak.

Broader in the shoulder than the other Snuffed Keepers. In one hand, a large lantern. The glass all broken, only the frame left.

In the other hand, a long pole. A pole for carrying a bier.

"Turn back."

It had a voice. Hoarse and cracked, but words.

The Keeper stopped.

"I've come to open the road."

"Turn back."

It held the pole crosswise.

"Anyone who carries a lantern becomes the same at the Door."

"I'll put it out."

The darkness inside the hood looked at the Keeper.

"He said that too."

The pole came.

It was long. It reached from a distance nothing should reach. The Keeper stepped back. Their back hit a brazier.

It did not strike the Keeper.

It struck the brazier.

The fire went out.

With the end of the pole, it swept the burning oil out across the floor. That brazier died.

Three braziers were within the pole's reach.

All three died.

The end of the hall went dark again.

The Keeper pulled back the hem of their cloak. The moths swarmed in. It didn't matter.

When the light reached it, it turned.

It stood the pole upright to guard. The dagger stuck in the wood. The scythe caught on the wood.

There was no opening.

The skill of one who had fought on this road longer than anyone. It knew every move the Keeper knew. It knew moves the Keeper didn't.

The pole struck their side. Where it had burst before.

The Keeper braced a hand on their knee.

"Turn back."

The third time.

This one had never once tried to kill. It had only tried to put out the lantern. To put out the braziers, and send the Keeper back.

For three hundred years.

"……I can't turn back."

The Keeper stood.

"Up above, the fires are going out. Eleven in a single night."

The pole faltered.

"He's pulling them. Even if you block the way here, he pulls at the world above. If no one comes down, every fire left up there will go out."

The darkness inside the hood did not move.

"Blocking the way won't end it."

It raised the pole.

Brought it down.

The Keeper did not dodge. They stepped inside. The pole grazed their shoulder and struck the floor.

They were inside its reach.

They could see the cord. The cord that ties body to soul.

Two more threads came out of it.

One was wound around this place, the end of the hall.

A thick one stretched through the gate and down. Taut.

That one could not be cut here. Only when it was cut from the far end would the piece come back.

The Keeper cut the thread wound around this place.

Cut the cord to the body.

The gray cloak fell to its knees.

The pole slipped from its hand and rolled.

It did not let go of the broken lantern to the very end.

---

A flame floated up.

A clear, straight flame.

When it came into the lantern, the glass rang.

It settled close beside the flame of the Lantern Keeper who had come in earlier. Facing the same way. Through the gate.

The Keeper caught their breath.

Blood seeped from their side again.

"I'll take you all the way."

They didn't know who they were saying it to. The flame that had come in, or the broken lantern.

"And find what was taken from you, too. Together."

They picked up the broken lantern and tied it to their belt. A place for the piece to come back to.

They looked back at the three dead braziers.

*Take it down a dark road, and it will lose its way.*

They scraped together the oil scattered on the floor and lit all three again.

There was little oil left in the lamp.

They held the lantern out through the gate.

The fire in the lantern stretched long.

Their own fire. The fire that must never go out.

In the hall, it had not been pulled this hard.

It was close.

And deep in the darkness beyond the gate, an amber light flickered.

This time it did not disappear.
