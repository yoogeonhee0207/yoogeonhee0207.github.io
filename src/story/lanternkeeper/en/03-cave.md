The tunnel was narrow.

Two people could hardly walk side by side. Reach up, and you could touch the ceiling.

Rotten wooden posts stood at even intervals. Pickaxe marks covered the walls like scales.

It was not the Funeral Road.

There were no braziers. No altar. Not one place for a Lantern Keeper to rest.

It looked like a tunnel someone had dug down from above. Its end had broken through halfway down the root road.

The Keeper looked again at the three soot lines at the mouth.

The teacher had stood here too. Had looked inside.

Had the teacher gone in?

There was no knowing.

From inside came that sound again.

Someone holding their breath.

The Keeper went into the tunnel.

---

About twenty steps in, they saw it.

Shaped like a person. Crouched with its back against the wall.

Covering its mouth with both hands.

With hands of bone, over a mouth that was already gone.

The light touched it, and still it did not move.

The soldiers of the catacombs had risen when they saw light. The guards of the root forest had raised their bows.

This one had frozen.

Like a child caught hiding.

The Keeper drew closer slowly. Drew the dagger.

Then, behind them, came the sound of earth underfoot.

They turned.

Three stood there. An arm's length away.

When the light touched them, all three froze where they were. Hands still reaching out.

A chill ran down the Keeper's spine.

Again, a sound from behind.

When they looked back, the crouching one had stood up. Had come a step closer.

It caught the light and froze again.

The Keeper pressed their back to the wall.

They thought they understood.

These could not move inside the light.

They came only from the side the light had turned away from.

*Don't chase them out of the light.*

Here, those words were not enough.

They came even if you didn't chase them. Every direction the lantern wasn't facing was outside the light.

The Keeper raised the lantern overhead. So the light spread evenly all around.

All four froze.

Only then did the Keeper look at them straight.

They wore no armor.

One gripped a pickaxe. One had a bundle on its back. One wore an apron.

And one was small.

It came up only to the Keeper's waist.

They were not soldiers, or guards.

Just people.

The Keeper's hand would not come down.

Cutting the cord was setting them free. The Keeper knew that. Had done it more than a hundred times.

Even so, their hand was heavy.

They cut the one with the pickaxe first. The earth crumbled. A flame floated up.

The one with the bundle.

The one in the apron.

In front of the small one, they knelt. Their eyes were level.

"I'm sorry."

They cut the cord.

A small flame came up. A little brighter than the others. It came into the lantern and went to the flame that had worn the apron, and stayed close.

For a long while, the Keeper could not get up.

---

The tunnel went deep.

They were at every fork.

The ones with pickaxes rushed all at once from the edge of the light. As if trying to reach you before the light did. They must have been the diggers.

From high ledges, stones flew. They must have been the lookouts who guarded the entrance.

And everywhere were the hushed ones.

In cracks in the walls. Behind a collapsed cart. In pits of standing water.

The Keeper walked with the lantern held overhead. Their arms went numb. Each time they switched hands, one side went dark, and each time, footsteps came.

There was a place that widened like a room.

A place where people had lived.

A hearth built of piled stones. Broken bowls. Old clothes hanging on the wall. Traces of straw once spread on the floor.

And lines drawn in charcoal on the wall.

They had counted days. Bundled in fives. Filling one whole wall, moving on to the next, and stopping partway along the one after.

The Keeper put a hand into the hearth.

There were no ashes.

A hearth that had never had a fire.

Then a voice came.

From a crack in the hearthstones. An old man's voice.

> When the buried rose, the people left the kingdom.
> Those of us who could not leave followed the Hollow Warden down into the old hollow.

The people who could not leave.

The Keeper looked at the small flame inside the lantern.

Deeper in, they heard once more.

> No Lantern Keeper came.
> There was no vigil fire to light for those who died in the hollow.

The Keeper stood still.

*No Lantern Keeper came.*

For three hundred years, the Lantern Keepers of the ridge had gone down this road. The teacher. The teacher's teacher.

All of them had passed the mouth of this tunnel.

Because the road had to be opened. Because only an open road could send everyone off.

It was not a wrong choice.

Even so, these people had waited three hundred years.

That was as much as the Keeper heard.

The voice remembered far more.

---

## The One Who Sealed the Door

We were the ones who could not leave.

Those without a cart. Those whose legs were not sound. Those with a babe at the breast. Those who could not leave old parents behind.

When the buried rose, the people left the kingdom.

Carts lined up and went out through the city gate. We only watched.

We were afraid of night coming.

At night, they came. Raise a torch and you saw nothing. Yet the person beside you fell.

The houses with fires lit were taken first.

Houses that had hung their ancestors' fires under the eaves. They came to those fires.

On the ridge there was a single lantern.

Only a few could fit inside its light. And we were people who came trailing the risen behind us.

The ridge did not open its door.

That was when he stepped forward.

A man who used to inspect the tunnels in the old mine. We called him the Hollow Warden.

"There's an old hollow up the mountain. One entrance. One, we can seal."

Those of us who could not leave followed the Hollow Warden down into the old hollow.

About two hundred of us.

At the mouth, the Hollow Warden stopped us one by one.

"Leave your fires behind."

Some had come carrying their ancestors' fires. Oil bowls wrapped close to their chests.

"They come to the fire. If there's even one fire in the hollow, we all die."

Some wept. The Hollow Warden did not give way.

We set the fires in a row on the mountainside and went in.

When the last person was inside, the Hollow Warden piled up stones.

From inside. He packed every crack with earth.

The Hollow Warden sealed the entrance.

"No sun reaches here, but it's better than what comes at night."

We believed him. It was all we had to believe.

---

The first few months, we got by.

There was water deep in the hollow. We had brought grain. The diggers cut further down. Looking for another way out.

We lit no fires.

Our eyes grew used to the dark. We knew who was who by each other's breathing.

We counted the days in charcoal on the wall.

We waited for a Lantern Keeper.

People said someone would come. That at least one must be left. That when the road opened again, they would come looking for us.

Once, there was a knocking on stone outside.

The Hollow Warden forbade us to answer. It might be them.

The sound did not come again.

No Lantern Keeper came.

In the first winter, an old man died.

We laid him deep in the hollow.

There was no fire to light for him.

There was no vigil fire to light for those who died in the hollow.

We had left our ancestors' fires on the mountainside. All we had in the hollow was flint. A soul won't take to a fire struck from flint. Even the children knew that.

"Open the entrance. I'll bring back just one fire."

The old man's son clung to the Hollow Warden.

The Hollow Warden shook his head.

"Do you think that fire is still burning? With no one to fill the oil?"

No one could answer.

Three nights later, there was a scraping of earth deep in the hollow.

---

The dead rose, one by one.

We couldn't see them. The hollow was dark anyway. There was only the sound.

Someone lit a torch.

Even under the torch, it could not be seen. Yet the one holding the torch fell.

It came toward the fire.

We put out the fire.

We hid from the light.

In cracks in the walls. Behind carts. In pits of water. Mouths covered. Breath held.

If you made no sound, it passed by.

That was how people lived.

When someone lit a fire, they came to it.

The Hollow Warden taught us, and it was the first thing we taught the children. When you see light, freeze. Don't breathe. Until the light passes.

Each time someone died, one more of the risen joined them.

We believed it was a sickness that spread. Spread by a bite. Spread by breath.

So we left the dying alone, deep in the hollow.

We didn't even hold their hands.

Now we know.

It did not spread.

Nor did it happen because we left them alone. Had we stayed beside them, they would have risen all the same.

There was no fire for them to take to. And it was we who left those fires on the mountainside.

For no reason at all, we sent the dying off alone.

---

There was a day we crowded to the entrance.

When fewer than fifty of us were left.

"Open it. We'll die outside. Let us at least see the sun before we die."

The Hollow Warden stood with his back to the pile of stones. Holding a pickaxe handle crosswise.

"I can't open it."

"Why not!"

"The things inside will get out."

The Hollow Warden's voice shook.

"There are still people in the village below. Those who stayed on the ridge. What gets out of here will go there."

We shoved him. He did not fall.

"If you want past me, kill me first."

No one could.

The Hollow Warden never opened the entrance.

For fear it would spread outside.

The last one left was the Hollow Warden.

We died one by one, and rose still hiding. Those who died in hiding came close only in the dark.

The Hollow Warden died sitting with his back against the stones. Gripping the pickaxe handle.

He rose with it still sealed.

---

After that, no one counted the days.

We still hide.

We have forgotten what we hide from. All that is left is that we must freeze when we see light. And that we must follow when it passes.

The light has passed by a few times.

Past the mouth of the hollow, down along the root road. One lantern at a time. Once a generation.

Once, it stopped at the mouth.

It looked inside for a long time. Drew something on the post, and went down again.

We watched it, frozen.

And this time, the light came into the hollow.

The smallest of us stood before that light.

The one holding the light knelt.

Said something.

We could not understand the words. We knew only that it was the first time in three hundred years we had heard them.

Words a person had spoken to us.

Now the light climbs the slope.

Toward where the Hollow Warden stands.

---

## The Sealed Entrance

The tunnel turned uphill.

The air changed. The smell of dry earth. And, very faintly, the smell of grass.

At the end was a pile of stones.

It blocked the whole tunnel. Earth packed into every crack. Hardened over three hundred years until it was like rock.

In front of it, it stood.

Large. Two heads taller than the others. Shoulders as wide as the tunnel.

Its back to the pile of stones.

Holding a pickaxe handle crosswise.

The light touched it.

It stopped too.

It was like the others in the tunnel. And it was not.

The others froze as if caught. This one held its head up. It looked straight into the light.

It had not frozen.

It was standing.

"I won't open it."

A voice like grinding stone.

The Keeper lowered the lantern.

"I'm not here to open it."

"If they get out, they'll go to the village."

Those two sayings were all it had left.

From its chest, threads stretched out. Dozens. Into the tunnel. Back along the way the Keeper had come.

Most were cut. The ends hung loose in the air.

The flames inside the lantern pressed against the glass. The flames gathered in the tunnel. Toward it.

Like the king in the catacombs. The one being held on to was it.

For three hundred years it had stood there, bearing all those threads.

The Keeper took a step closer.

It did not move.

Another step.

Behind, footsteps.

The ones still left. While the lantern faced forward, they had been creeping up behind.

The Keeper turned the lantern. Three froze an arm's length away.

Behind them, the earth shook.

There was no time to turn. They raised the scythe. The pickaxe handle struck the scythe and came down onto their shoulder. The shoulder cut in the root forest.

Everything went white.

In the one beat the light was turned away, it had come three steps.

They swung the lantern forward again. It stopped. Handle still raised.

From behind, a hand grabbed their cloak.

Light the front, and the back came. Light the back, and the front came.

Holding it overhead would do it. Their arm would not rise. The shoulder that had been struck.

*Never let the fire go out.*

The Keeper set the lantern on the ground. In the middle of the tunnel. So the light spread evenly front and back.

Both hands were free.

The ones behind froze. The hand gripping the cloak froze too. The Keeper tore the hem and slipped free.

The Hollow Warden stood as well.

The Keeper walked right up to it. Within reach of the handle.

It did not strike.

With the scythe, the Keeper cleared away the threads. The few still left.

Each time one was cut, something crumbled behind them.

One last strand remained.

What remained was the Hollow Warden's own cord. Thick. It came from the body and wound all the way to the pile of stones. It held not only its body, but those stones too.

The Keeper set the dagger to it.

It moved.

In the middle of the light.

It had been standing from the start. Cutting that cord meant opening the entrance. When there was something to stop, it moved. As it had for three hundred years.

The handle came down on the Keeper's back.

Falling, the Keeper drew the dagger across.

It parted.

---

The pickaxe handle dropped to the floor.

The large body leaned against the stones and slowly sank down. Sitting, it turned to earth.

A flame floated up.

Not large. Not large like the king's. An ordinary person's flame. Only very dim.

The flame did not leave the pile of stones.

It was still guarding it.

The Keeper crawled over and picked up the lantern. Opened the little door.

"Everyone is out."

They were out of breath.

"There's no one left inside. I'm taking them all with me."

The flame did not move.

"The village is safe."

The Keeper chose the words.

"There's a village still left up above. It's been safe for three hundred years. That's where I come from."

The flame wavered.

"It's thanks to you holding the door."

Whether that was true, there was no knowing. What would have happened if the things in the tunnel had gotten out, no one could know.

But it was not a lie.

The flame came away from the stones.

Slowly it came into the lantern.

The flames gathered in the tunnel made way for it. Then gathered again, and surrounded that dim flame.

They did not push it away.

The Keeper sank down in front of the stones.

Their back hurt. Their shoulder hurt. Their side hurt.

They reached out and touched a stone.

The earth crumbled.

One stone shifted.

They pushed.

The stone slid out the other side.

Light came in.

A thin white line of light, straight into the tunnel.

Whiter than lantern light.

The sun.

The first sun to enter this tunnel in three hundred years.

The Keeper covered their eyes. Looked through their fingers.

Through the gap they could see a mountainside. Dry grass. And below it, roofs.

The village on the ridge.

The pile of stones on the slope behind the village. The place the children never went near.

This was the inside of it.

The beam of light fell on the tunnel floor. On the heap of earth there. The earth that had been the Hollow Warden a moment ago.

The Keeper put their eye to the gap and looked at the village a long time.

They could just go out.

Push a few more stones and they could get out. Drink water, bind their wounds, sleep a night.

They could not tell how many days had passed.

They could see the fires hanging under the eaves.

Lit even in daytime. A few houses were empty. More empty eaves than the day they left.

The Keeper took their eye from the gap.

They did not put the stone back.

They let the sun in.

Then they picked up the lantern and went back down the way they had come.

The root road went on downward.

From its end rose the smell of something burning.

The smell of a fire that went out long ago.
